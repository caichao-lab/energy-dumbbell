# Energy Dumbbell Business Philosophy: A Rule‑Engine‑Based Marketing Decision Theory Framework




## Abstract

This paper proposes the Energy Business Philosophy (EBP), a rule‑engine‑based marketing decision theory framework. The system is built on the core ontological assumption that "business is an energy field" and the practical proposition that "operators are entropy‑resisters," constructing a five‑dimensional theoretical framework encompassing ontology, epistemology, kinetics, axiology, and praxeology. The system adopts a three‑layer spatial structure of "Energy Planet ‑ Dumbbell Body ‑ Y‑Branch Units," with "Seven‑Rate Diagnosis ‑ Five‑Valve Regulation ‑ Three‑Layer Wave Diffusion" as operational tools, "Operation Entity / Idle Entity dual‑system isolation" as governance mechanism, and the "Wine Glass Rotation" positive‑negative cycle as dynamic model. This paper elaborates on the theoretical origins, core concepts, reasoning mechanisms, and application scenarios of the system, and discusses its complementary value to existing marketing theories and its practical significance for the development of AI marketing tools.

**Keywords**: Energy Business Philosophy; Rule Engine; Marketing Decision; Entropy Resistance; Y‑Branch Unit; Five‑Valve Regulation



## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Research Background

Artificial intelligence is reshaping the field of marketing decision‑making. Large language models (LLMs), represented by the GPT series, are capable of generating seemingly reasonable marketing strategy recommendations through massive data training. However, these tools suffer from three fundamental flaws:

First, **inexplicability**. The reasoning process of LLMs is a black box. Decision‑makers cannot know the basis of strategy recommendations, leading to "reluctance to use."

Second, **non‑verifiability**. Probabilistic generation mechanisms may produce different outputs from the same input, compromising the consistency and reliability of strategies.

Third, **compliance risk**. Generative AI services face increasingly stringent regulatory filing requirements. Some platforms have already taken down related applications, exposing enterprises to the risk of tool supply disruption.

### 1.2 Research Questions

This paper attempts to answer: Is there a **explainable, verifiable, and compliance‑safe** marketing decision theory framework that can replace or complement existing LLM‑based solutions?

### 1.3 Research Contributions

This paper proposes the Energy Business Philosophy (EBP), with theoretical contributions in four dimensions:

First, **ontological innovation**. Shifting business from "mechanism" (enterprise as machine) to "energism" (enterprise as energy field), introducing the "Y‑Branch Unit" as the fundamental particle of resources.

Second, **epistemological innovation**. Shifting marketing diagnosis from "experiential intuition" to "measurement‑cybernetics," constructing the operable system of "Seven‑Rate Metrics – Five‑Valve Regulation – Three‑Layer Wave Diffusion."

Third, **kinetic innovation**. Shifting business growth from "linear growth theory" to "spiral cycle theory," proposing the "Wine Glass Rotation" positive‑negative cycle model.

Fourth, **governance innovation**. Shifting "core business" and "idle resources" from mixed management to "dual‑system isolation," proposing the separate governance mechanism of Operation Entity / Idle Entity.



## 2. Theoretical Origins and Literature Review

### 2.1 The Evolution of Marketing Theory

#### 2.1.1 Classical Marketing Theory (1950s‑1980s)

- **4P Theory** (McCarthy, 1960): static framework of Product, Price, Place, Promotion
- **STP Theory** (Kotler, 1967): strategic process of Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning
- **Service Marketing 7P** (Booms & Bitner, 1981): extended framework adding People, Process, Physical Evidence

**Limitation**: static framework, lacking dynamic regulation mechanisms.

#### 2.1.2 Relationship Marketing and CRM (1990s‑2000s)

- **Relationship Marketing** (Berry, 1983): shifting from transactions to relationships
- **CRM Theory** (Payne & Frow, 2005): customer lifetime value management

**Limitation**: focuses on customer relationships, lacking a full‑chain resource allocation perspective.

#### 2.1.3 Digital Marketing and Big Data (2010s‑2020s)

- **Precision Marketing** (Chen et al., 2012): personalized recommendation based on user profiles
- **Growth Hacking** (Ellis & Brown, 2017): data‑driven rapid experimentation
- **AI Marketing** (Davenport et al., 2020): machine learning for marketing decision optimization

**Limitation**: data‑quality dependent, black‑box reasoning, compliance risks.

### 2.2 Systems Science and Management Cybernetics

#### 2.2.1 General System Theory (Bertalanffy, 1968)

Open systems, entropy and negentropy, system hierarchy.

**Connection to EBP**: EBP's "energy field" assumption directly inherits from system theory; the concept of "entropy resistance" originates from negentropy theory.

#### 2.2.2 Management Cybernetics (Beer, 1959; Forrester, 1961)

Viable System Model (VSM), system dynamics.

**Connection to EBP**: EBP's "Five‑Valve Regulation" is a concrete application of management cybernetics in marketing; "Three‑Layer Wave Diffusion" is the wave expression of system dynamics.

### 2.3 Physics Metaphors in Management

#### 2.3.1 Organizational Ecology (Hannan & Freeman, 1977)

Population ecology, selection‑adaptation‑evolution.

#### 2.3.2 Complex Adaptive Systems (Holland, 1995)

Emergence, self‑organization, adaptive agents.

#### 2.3.3 EBP's Innovation in Physics Metaphors

Existing research predominantly borrows biological metaphors (ecology, evolution). EBP is the first to systematically introduce **physics metaphors** (energy field, particles, waves, entropy, pressure differentials), constructing a complete "business physics" language system.

### 2.4 Literature Review Synthesis

Existing marketing theories have three gaps:

1. **Static vs. Dynamic**: frameworks are plentiful, but regulation mechanisms are scarce
2. **Holistic vs. Partial**: full‑chain perspectives are rare; single‑point optimizations are common
3. **Black‑Box vs. White‑Box**: explainable theoretical tools are scarce

EBP attempts to fill these three gaps.
